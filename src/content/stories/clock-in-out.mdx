Caregivers clock in when they arrive and clock out when they leave. It sounds
trivial. It is not — those two timestamps feed compliance, payroll, and billing,
so they have to be **reliable, auditable, and correct** even when the network
isn't cooperating.

## Why a microservice

Attendance has its own lifecycle and its own reliability needs, distinct from the
rest of the platform. Pulling it into a dedicated **Dapr** microservice meant it
could be reasoned about, scaled, and hardened on its own — and integrated with
the wider system through clean, event-driven contracts rather than shared
database tables.

## The Dapr building blocks

Dapr's job here was to provide the boring-but-essential plumbing so I could focus
on the domain:

- **Pub/sub** to publish attendance events the rest of the platform reacts to.
- **State** for the service's own durable data.
- **Resilient service invocation** with retries and timeouts baked in.

## Two things that always bite you

- **Duplicates.** With at-least-once delivery, the same clock-in event can show
  up twice. Every handler is **idempotent** — keyed so a replay changes nothing.
- **Time.** Caregivers span US states, timezones, and daylight-saving changes.
  Store UTC, keep the originating timezone alongside it, and render local. (There
  is a bug in the [Graveyard](/archipelago) about the day I learned this the hard
  way.)

## Designed out loud

A surprising amount of this was decided at a whiteboard. I worked through the
design in architecture-review sessions with senior engineers and product
stakeholders — which is where I learned that the best distributed-systems
decisions are made *before* any code exists, by arguing about failure modes
until the design stops having them.
