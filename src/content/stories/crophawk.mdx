Before the microservices, there were drones. Starting in 2022, as an R&D
Engineer at JUIT, I spent the better part of two years building **CropHawk** — an
agri-drone prototype that went on to secure funding from **Kanpur Fertiliser &
Chemical Ltd (KFCL)** in 2023 — building the systems that let an aircraft look at
a field and understand what it was seeing.

## The whole stack, from rotor to report

"R&D Engineer" on a small team means you touch everything. I worked across:

- **Custom drone frameworks** for autonomous flight and mission planning in real
  farms, with dynamic path adjustment mid-flight.
- **On-device (edge) ML** so crop-health analysis ran *on the drone, on the go* —
  no cloud round-trip, no latency, decisions in the field.
- **Multi-spectral image processing** to spot crop-health issues and nutrient
  deficiencies early, before they're visible to the eye.
- **IoT and robotics integration**, streaming high-frequency sensor and aerial
  data into the analytics behind it.

This is where I got my hands genuinely dirty with **embedded systems** and
end-to-end product development — and learned how unforgiving the physical world
is compared to a server.

## The lab lies

The single biggest lesson: a model that aces clean lab data can fall apart the
moment real sunlight, dust, and motion enter the frame. Edge ML for agriculture
is as much about **validating against messy field reality** as it is about the
algorithm. (That story has its own headstone in the
[Graveyard of Bugs](/archipelago).)

## A paper to show for it

The custom algorithm we built for real-time, drone-based agricultural analysis
became a **research paper that I published and presented at IC3SE**, an
international conference. Working directly with the client and stakeholders to
turn their requirements into something that flies — and then defending the
results in front of a research audience — is still some of the most complete
engineering I've done.
