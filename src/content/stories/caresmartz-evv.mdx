Electronic Visit Verification (EVV) is the unglamorous backbone of home
healthcare: proof that a caregiver was where they were supposed to be, when they
were supposed to be there. In the US it's also a compliance minefield — every
state aggregator has its own rules, formats, and deadlines. CareSmartz360 had to
satisfy **13 of them**, and for a long time that logic lived inside one large
.NET monolith.

## The problem with one big box

A monolith is fine until it isn't. Ours coupled 13 states' worth of integration
logic into a single deployable. A change for one state meant redeploying — and
risking — all of them. Failures didn't isolate; a bad day for one aggregator
could ripple. Scaling meant scaling everything at once.

## Breaking it apart, carefully

The goal wasn't a rewrite — it was a **gradual extraction** into independently
deployable .NET microservices, so each integration could change and scale on its
own. The hard part of this kind of work is never the new service; it's keeping
the old system working while you carve pieces off it.

To make the pieces talk without tightly re-coupling them, we leaned on
[Dapr](https://dapr.io):

- **Pub/sub** for event-driven messaging, so services react to events instead of
  calling each other directly.
- **State stores** for durable, service-owned state.
- **Resilient invocation** with retries, so transient failures don't become
  outages.

## Workflows that can't just fail halfway

EVV processes are *long-running* and *compliance-critical* — exactly the kind of
work you can't leave half-done. We modelled those with **Camunda** (BPMN for
processes, CMMN for case-style flows) and used **Apache Airflow** for scheduled,
data-heavy orchestration. The point of a workflow engine here is durability: the
process survives restarts and knows how to resume.

Two distributed-systems disciplines did most of the heavy lifting:

- **Idempotency.** In an at-least-once world, every message can arrive twice.
  Consumers are written so a duplicate is a no-op, not a double-charge.
- **Fault tolerance.** Pipelines retry with backoff and dead-letter what they
  can't process, instead of retrying forever.

> The lesson I keep relearning: in distributed systems, "it worked once" and "it
> works reliably" are completely different claims.

## Healthcare-grade data

Healthcare data has rules. I went through **HIPAA compliance training and
certification**, then helped integrate workflows with **Azure Health Data
Services (FHIR)** — building compliant pipelines on Azure Functions and Blob
Storage for data exchange. Separately, I helped migrate analytical reports off a
legacy .NET reporting engine onto **Power BI**, which made the numbers both
prettier and more maintainable for business users.

## Sharpening the toolchain

A quieter thread through all of this: better ways of building. I've been
exploring **spec-first / constitution-driven development** with GitHub's Spec
Kit — pairing AI-assisted coding with human validation and security review — and
relying on tools like Copilot, SonarQube, and NotebookLM to move faster without
lowering the bar on quality.

It's the most systems-heavy work I've done, and the most rewarding: you're not
shipping a feature, you're shaping how a whole platform communicates.
